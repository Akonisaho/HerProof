## 1. Positioning

**One-liner:** HerProof lets a woman prove what she can do, with evidence an employer or bursary can trust,
whether or not she has a degree, a CV, or an unbroken career.

**Tagline:** Her skills. Her proof. Her future.

**Problem statement answered:** "Unequal access to opportunity: how might women find accessible pathways
to education, employment, leadership and economic independence?"
Also touches: *Unpaid care and time pressure* (Beyond Employment recognises caregiving skills) and
*Digital access and skills gaps* (learning pathways, low-bandwidth resilience, partner hubs for device access).

**The sentence that frames everything:** Existing tools help employers screen candidates.
HerProof helps candidates prove themselves.

## 7. Judge Q&A

**"HackerEarth already does AI interviews."** They screen for employers from question banks. Our interviewer questions
the candidate about her own repository, the result goes to a human assessor, and the output is a credential she owns
and a bursary can use. Different customer, different outcome.

**"Can't candidates cheat with AI?"** No single signal is trusted. The repo interview tests whether she can explain
her code. The live assessment tests whether she can produce work. Integrity events go to a human. And we say
"not demonstrated through this evidence," not "cheating."

**"Isn't monitoring invasive?"** It records a short, published list of events, shows them to the candidate live,
never captures screens or other apps, never auto-fails, and offers accommodations.

**"What about bias in AI?"** The AI never scores accent, face, emotion or fluency. Candidates can correct transcripts.
There is a text-only path with the same rubric. A human makes every verification decision.

**"Women without laptops or data?"** Honest limit: the Electron assessment needs a Windows/macOS/Linux machine.
Plan: partner hubs (training programmes, libraries) host sessions, autosave tolerates drops, and the interview
has a low-bandwidth text mode. [Add any partner letters of intent you actually have.]

**"POPIA?"** Consent per purpose, minimisation, retention schedule, access and deletion requests, operator
agreements with AI and speech providers, and a legal review before launch. Cross-border processing
needs a lawful basis, so provider region and terms matter.

**"Who pays?"** Employers, bursary funders, and training institutions. Candidates never pay to be recognised.

**"What did you actually build in the hackathon?"** Be exact. Say which parts are live and which are stubbed.
Judges forgive stubs. They do not forgive faking.

**"Why not just use a certificate?"** Certificates record attendance or exam results. A passport records
demonstrated competencies, with method, rubric, assessor and evidence, and expires when it should.
